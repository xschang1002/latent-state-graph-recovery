Grouping local graph estimates can change structural ranking and temporal
recovery differently. We operationalise known ranking principles through a
controlled protocol separating pooled AUROC, within-edge state discrimination
and boundary detection, with a task-specific two-state diagnostic identity.
In 100 independent paired simulations, hard aggregation changes state AUROC
by $-0.069$ and $+0.038$ at two duration configurations; changing duration
also changes switch coverage. A separate symmetric-generator study reproduces
the contrast at the original window configuration, with point-estimate
exceptions whose intervals cross zero elsewhere.
Non-clustering shrinkage improves pooled ranking without changing temporal
order. Residual accounting shows that near-zero positive weights can
recover within-group ranking by breaking hard-output ties. Boundary conclusions
also depend on zero-score treatment and amplitude reference: standard AP and
method-relative thresholds change assessments based on positive-change AP and
a shared raw threshold. A PELT plus segment-VAR comparator likewise improves
pooled AUROC without establishing a state-ranking gain, extending the protocol
beyond recurrent clustering and its gate. These findings concern the tested
two-state linear systems, not a general duration threshold. Complete retained
outputs and selected from-scratch reruns support reproducibility; static
industrial alignment remains a separate validation target.
