## The four specified sources

**Clémençon, Lugosi and Vayatis.** The requested [arXiv paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/math/0603123), Section 2, Example 1 (PDF pp. 5–6), identifies increasing transformations of the class posterior as optimal bipartite-ranking scores. The final article is *The Annals of Statistics* **36**(2), 844–874 (2008), DOI [10.1214/009052607000000910](https://doi.org/10.1214/009052607000000910). The current DOI is correct; `009053...` is not a replacement supported by this audit. In the present finite target, the feature can be the edge identity and its posterior is its occupancy. The paper's occupancy bound is therefore a useful application of existing optimal-ranking theory. This source does not itself establish the present graph-specific two-emission factorisation, the measured gate effects or the empirical ordering of methods. Its tie convention must not silently replace the manuscript's half-credit convention.

**Shakil, Lee and Keilholz.** The requested [author manuscript](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4889509/) has the final citation *NeuroImage* **133**, 111–128 (2016), DOI [10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.02.074](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neuroimage.2016.02.074). The abstract, “Effects of window offset” and Discussion “Window offset” explicitly examine sliding-window correlations followed by k-means against known simulated configurations and transitions. Window length and offset affect recovered transitions and durations. The study is about undirected functional connectivity; it is a direct methodological antecedent, not evidence that directed lagged-support recovery has already been evaluated using this manuscript's targets.

**scikit-learn AP documentation.** The requested [official page](https://scikit-learn.org/stable/modules/generated/sklearn.metrics.average_precision_score.html), definition and Notes, defines non-interpolated AP as the sum of precision weighted by recall increments. It distinguishes this from trapezoidal PR area. The live page was version 1.9.1 when inspected; that is a documentation version, not a claim about the environment used in the experiments. No publication year is inferred from the page footer.

Let `a >= 0` be the change strengths on one eligible edge, `P > 0` the number of positive slots, `n` the number of slots, `pi=P/n`, and `R_+` the recall reached by strictly positive strengths. With the same whole-tie-group convention, the final zero-strength group in ordinary AP has precision `pi` and recall increment `1-R_+`. Therefore

\[
\mathrm{AP}_{\mathrm{standard}}
=\mathrm{AP}_{+}+\pi(1-R_{+}).
\]

This is a direct derivation from the standard definition, rather than a new theorem attributed to the documentation. All-zero strengths have ordinary AP equal to prevalence, while the current abstention statistic is zero. The identity does not override the declared exclusion of targets lacking either class; report those exclusions explicitly. For positive shrinkage, exact-arithmetic change strengths scale by the positive weight, so both AP rankings are preserved, whereas fixed-amplitude F1 need not be.

**ESS benchmark.** The requested [official PMLR page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/mogensen24a.html) and [paper first page](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v236/mogensen24a/mogensen24a.pdf) name **Søren Wengel Mogensen, Karin Rathsman and Per Nilsson**. The canonical proceedings citation is *Proceedings of the Third Conference on Causal Learning and Reasoning*, PMLR **236**, 1218–1236 (2024). The benchmark provides expert-constructed graph knowledge for an industrial system. This source does not turn the present static expert-edge alignment into validation of a change-edge timing detector.

## Residual controls and contribution language

For `R_lambda=(1-lambda)H+lambda S`, where `H` is a hard group emission and `S` the raw score, a pair inside one hard group satisfies

\[
R_\lambda(t)-R_\lambda(u)=\lambda\{S(t)-S(u)\}.
\]

Thus every positive weight restores the raw order of that pair, while weight zero makes it a tie. Across groups the difference is `(1-lambda) Delta H + lambda Delta S`; order can change at `lambda=Delta H/(Delta H-Delta S)` when that value lies in the tested range. A near-zero jump in AUROC is compatible with this tie structure. Its empirical size requires the saved pair counts; it cannot be inferred from the formula alone. Finite-precision ties must be measured rather than repaired.

The following three contribution statements keep the levels of evidence distinct. The comparator and fresh-fit clauses should be retained only after their results have actually been produced and checked.

1. **Evaluation targets and task-specific diagnostics.** We organise dynamic graph recovery by its evaluation target and indexing. Applying established optimal-ranking principles to finite edge occupancies, and separating same-edge from cross-edge comparisons, explains which temporal claims pooled structural rankings can and cannot support.
2. **Mechanisms behind changes in the measured ranking.** For two-state, two-map hard emission, an exact factorisation connects state-discrimination AUROC to grouping and graph contrast. Score-preserving shrinkage, within-group residual ordering and the rejection output distinguish state identification from ranking changes caused by compression or tie breaking. These conditional identities do not establish an optimal algorithm or a universal aggregation benefit.
3. **Controlled empirical consequences.** Paired experiments compare the resulting structural, within-edge state and boundary-slot conclusions across specified duration/schedule/window conditions, a separate symmetric generator, and a distinct segment-refit construction. Standard-AP and threshold sensitivity delimit amplitude-dependent conclusions. These are empirical tests of the evaluation account; prototype-specific gate behaviour and fresh-fit checks retain their own scope.

The contribution is stronger if the protocol changes the interpretation of a representative comparator, rather than merely illustrating a mathematical identity on the prototype. Conversely, a comparator that yields a different pattern is useful evidence about scope and should not be forced into a universal narrative. No sign or significance of the new PELT or sensitivity results is predicted here.

## PELT implementation and citation support

The [official PELT source reference](https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/code-reference/detection/pelt-reference/) shows `min_size=2`, `jump=5` as defaults, candidate subsampling by `jump`, and inclusion of `n_samples` as the final endpoint. The [user guide](https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/user-guide/detection/pelt/) describes the segmentation algorithm. A new experiment using `jump=1` and `min_size=2` should state those explicit choices, its cost and penalty, and how segment boundaries map to the later refit. PELT supplies segmentation; graph estimation by refitting inside its segments is an additional pipeline component.

The [official project homepage](https://centre-borelli.github.io/ruptures-docs/) recommends citing Charles Truong, Laurent Oudre and Nicolas Vayatis, *Selective review of offline change point detection methods*, *Signal Processing* **167**, 107299 (2020), DOI [10.1016/j.sigpro.2019.107299](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.sigpro.2019.107299). That is the project's recommended software citation and a review, not a replacement attribution for the original Killick–Fearnhead–Eckley algorithm.
