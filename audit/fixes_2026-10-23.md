## Corrected ESS protocol and result

Command: `python scripts/run_ess_alignment.py --all-imputations`.
All four imputations, four methods and two expert edge sets were rerun (32 rows).
Every PELT fit detects 23 segments and refits 23 disjoint ranges. The former
forward-fill result was exactly the global Lasso fit; its all-edge AUROC changes
from 0.576290 to 0.564972 and strong-edge AUROC from 0.657464 to 0.554761.

The degree null fixes each subsystem's labelled in/out degree. A proposal selects
a pair of existing edges and exchanges destinations (90%), or selects a vertex
triple and reverses an isolated directed cycle (10%). Invalid proposals retain
the current graph, rather than advancing only on accepted moves. This symmetric
proposal scheme has the uniform degree-constrained graph distribution as a
stationary distribution; finite-chain output is approximate. Per edge set:
seed 0, 200 retained samples, 100|E| burn-in proposals, 10|E| proposals between
samples, with the same graphs used for all methods. Methods and both edge sets
are unchanged. The upper-tail p-value is `(1 + exceedances) / 201`.

The primary family remains the eight forward-fill tests. Other imputations remain
exploratory. For reference, all 32 tests are also adjusted together.

| Forward-fill comparison | AUROC | Raw p | Holm p (8 tests) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| STCG, all edges | 0.570963 | 0.014925 | 0.104478 |
| PELT + refit, all edges | 0.564972 | 0.099502 | 0.398010 |
| PELT + refit, strong edges | 0.554761 | 0.124378 | 0.398010 |
| Lagged correlation, strong edges | 0.677173 | 0.004975 | 0.039801 |

Lagged correlation on strong edges is the sole primary-family rejection at 0.05.
No comparison survives the combined 32-test family. This supersedes the earlier
no-rejection statement; it remains an exploratory alignment result conditional on
the null and on the coarse 1/201 Monte Carlo resolution. It is not graph truth.
The all-edge leader also depends on imputation: STCG for forward fill, PELT for
backward fill, lagged correlation for linear and mean imputation.
